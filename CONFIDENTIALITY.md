# Confidentiality Policy

## Purpose

This portfolio is a private, curated evidence room showing how Christopher Swarup approaches revenue systems, GTM operations, martech, analytics, AI workflows and venture building.

It is not a storage location for historic employer documents, company data, customer information or personal records.

## Core rule

> Public career facts may be named. All operating evidence must be independently reconstructed, company-neutral and free from real customer or employee data.

## Information classes

### 1. Portfolio-ready

Material created specifically for this portfolio using original wording, neutral terminology and synthetic examples.

Examples:

- Independently written operating frameworks
- Newly created diagrams
- Synthetic datasets
- Generic process models
- High-level venture case studies based on owned work

### 2. Rebuild before use

Source material that contains useful professional learning but cannot be copied into GitHub.

Examples:

- Historic operating models
- Internal process presentations
- CRM field definitions
- Governance manuals
- Dashboards and reports
- Transformation plans

The underlying idea may be extracted, but the portfolio version must be rebuilt from scratch.

### 3. Restricted source

Material that may inform private analysis but must never appear in a repository.

Examples:

- Internal company data
- Customer, prospect, employee, partner or supplier information
- Screenshots from production systems
- Contracts, pricing or commercial terms
- Security designs
- Internal URLs or system identifiers
- Exact proprietary configuration
- Internal metrics, budgets, customer volumes or performance data
- Named internal programmes, objects, fields, workflows or incidents

### 4. Never include

- Passwords, API keys, tokens or credentials
- Recovery codes or backup codes
- Identity documents
- Payroll, tax or banking records
- Personal contact information or precise home location
- Personal family information
- Customer lists, prospect lists or contact exports
- Employee records
- Signed contracts or legal documents belonging to a third party
- Production data of any kind

## Named-company rule

Public employer names, public role titles and public employment dates may appear only in the factual career timeline.

A named company must not be connected in reviewer-facing material to:

- Internal metrics or budgets
- Customer or employee information
- Private product or programme names
- Internal system architecture
- Proprietary field names or process labels
- Exact tool combinations where identifying
- Internal incidents, weaknesses or failures
- Confidential migration, acquisition or integration detail
- Unreleased strategy, pricing or commercial terms

All case studies, framework examples, skill demonstrations and transformation narratives must use neutral descriptions such as:

- Global B2B technology company
- Multi-region enterprise
- High-growth SaaS business
- Post-merger commercial organisation
- Product-led and sales-led revenue model

## Employer-anonymity rules

An anonymised case study must not reveal an employer through indirect clues.

Remove or generalise:

- Company and product names
- Internal programme names
- Employee names and identifiable job titles
- Exact dates and chronology where unnecessary
- Exact technology combinations if uniquely revealing
- Exact financial figures, volumes, headcounts and team ratios
- Internal terminology, field names and process labels
- Branded templates, fonts, colours and screenshots
- Customer segments, regions or acquisition combinations that would identify the organisation

## Customer and employee data

No real customer, prospect, employee, partner, supplier, parent, child or user record may be used in this repository.

Examples must use:

- Fictional organisations
- Fictional people
- Clearly synthetic domains
- Generated records
- Generic field names
- Non-identifying timestamps and values

Real data must not be anonymised and reused. It must be replaced with synthetic data.

## Personal-information rules

The repository must not contain:

- Personal email addresses
- Personal telephone numbers
- Home addresses or precise location
- Family or child information
- Identity, health, tax, banking, payroll or legal records
- Private social messages
- Personal photographs unless separately approved and metadata-reviewed

Professional education, certifications and public career history may be stated without attaching private source documents.

## Metrics

Use exact metrics only where the figure is already public, clearly owned, independently verified and safe to disclose.

Otherwise use:

- Rounded values
- Broad ranges
- Indexed improvements
- Directional outcomes
- Qualitative evidence with clear limitations

Never invent an outcome or expose a former employer through a distinctive combination of metric, date, system and event.

## Venture boundaries

### Lynr

Lynr may be named because it is an owned venture. Private financial information, customer information and commercially sensitive delivery details remain excluded unless explicitly approved.

### ThinkBud

ThinkBud may be represented through a high-level product-building case study.

The portfolio process must not:

- Modify the ThinkBud repository without explicit instruction
- Copy ThinkBud source code into this organisation
- Copy production configuration or secrets
- Expose child, parent, customer or user data
- Reproduce proprietary implementation details unnecessarily

### Other ventures

A venture may be named only where Christopher owns or controls the relevant intellectual property and the material has passed the review checklist.

## Recipient expectations

This repository is private and shared selectively. Access does not grant permission to redistribute, publish or reuse the material.

## Required review

Before any new file is committed:

1. Confirm ownership or permitted use.
2. Limit named-company information to public career facts.
3. Remove identifying employer, customer, employee and partner information.
4. Replace real data with synthetic data.
5. Check hidden metadata and embedded links.
6. Run the repository review checklist.
7. Run secret and personal-data scans.
8. Search for employer, customer and internal programme names.
9. Record the source treatment in `SOURCE-PROVENANCE.md`.
10. Reject the artefact if safe reconstruction would still reveal the source organisation.
