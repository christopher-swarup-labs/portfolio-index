# Google Drive Evidence Audit

**Audit date:** 18 July 2026  
**Purpose:** Identify the complete body of portfolio-worthy work, classify its evidential value, and prevent confidential, personal, third-party or proprietary material from entering the portfolio.

## Audit conclusion

The Drive contains a substantial body of work spanning more than a decade. It supports a portfolio far broader than a small collection of lifecycle, martech and venture case studies.

The strongest through-line is the design and operation of commercial systems across five connected layers:

1. Commercial motions
2. Process and ownership
3. Platforms and integration
4. Data and governance
5. Analytics, automation and AI

The evidence also demonstrates team leadership, transformation delivery, enablement, programme governance, business-case development and venture creation.

The portfolio must therefore be structured as an **operator evidence system**, not as a collection of old presentations.

## Evidence classifications

| Classification | Meaning | Portfolio treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Validated operator evidence** | The material shows original diagnosis, design, implementation, governance or leadership work | Reconstruct as an anonymised case study, framework or tool |
| **Supporting evidence** | The material supports context, chronology or breadth but is not strong enough alone | Use to strengthen a case, not as the centrepiece |
| **Independent strategic exercise** | Work created for an assessment, interview or hypothetical business situation | Label clearly as an independent exercise; never imply implementation |
| **Owned-venture evidence** | Strategy, product, brand, website or operating work for a venture owned or built by Christopher | May be named, subject to venture-specific confidentiality review |
| **Third-party reference** | Training, templates, research or external intellectual property | Never present as original work; cite only where needed |
| **Never include** | Credentials, personal data, contracts, production exports, private records or proprietary source material | Exclude completely |

## Evidence that requires careful reconstruction

The following source classes are valuable but cannot be copied directly:

- Employer-branded operating manuals
- Detailed system architecture and field maps
- Internal lifecycle and routing rules
- Confidential transformation decks
- Internal QBRs and performance reports
- Production screenshots
- Real customer, employee or prospect records
- Exact platform URLs, object names and system identifiers
- Commercially sensitive budgets, forecasts and performance figures
- Merger, migration and organisational-change materials

Treatment:

- Extract the operating problem and design pattern
- Rewrite in company-neutral language
- Replace real data with synthetic examples
- Generalise or independently verify metrics
- Remove names, dates, logos, URLs and distinctive internal terminology
- Explain the reasoning and trade-offs rather than reproducing the source artefact

## Metrics rule

Some source documents contain performance claims and interview narratives. These are not automatically portfolio-safe facts.

Before a metric is used, it must be classified as one of:

- **Verified:** supported by a reliable source and safe to disclose
- **Generalised:** expressed as a range or directional outcome
- **Context only:** used privately to understand impact but not published
- **Unverified:** excluded until evidence is available

No exact employer metric should be added merely because it appears in a CV, interview deck or retrospective document.

## Third-party intellectual property boundary

The Drive includes training materials, vendor decks, analyst frameworks, templates and copied reference documents.

These materials may inform learning history, but they are not evidence of original authorship. They must not be copied into the portfolio or represented as Christopher’s frameworks.

Where an original portfolio framework has been influenced by established external thinking, the portfolio should:

- Use original structure and language
- Attribute the relevant external concept where material
- Explain Christopher’s applied judgement or extension
- Avoid reproducing proprietary templates

## Coverage gaps identified

The Drive is rich in source material but the portfolio still needs independently reconstructed proof in several forms:

- Clear before-and-after case narratives
- Decision logs showing trade-offs
- Synthetic datasets and runnable diagnostics
- Implementation artefacts stripped of company context
- Adoption and change-management evidence
- Explicit lessons, failures and design revisions
- Role clarity separating personal contribution from team output
- Verification or generalisation of claimed outcomes

These are portfolio-production gaps, not experience gaps.

## Portfolio architecture required by this audit

The evidence should be organised into five collections:

### 1. Operating systems

Reusable frameworks for revenue, lifecycle, campaign, martech, data and governance.

### 2. Transformation case studies

Anonymised stories showing diagnosis, decisions, delivery, adoption and outcomes.

### 3. Leadership systems

Team charters, service models, prioritisation, stakeholder governance, enablement and operating cadence.

### 4. Runnable proof

Synthetic validators, scorecards, diagnostics and workflow prototypes.

### 5. Venture builds

Owned-business and product stories covering market, offer, product, brand, commercial model and execution.
