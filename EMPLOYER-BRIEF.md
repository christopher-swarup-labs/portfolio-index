## How I work

I start with the **decision**, not the software.

I make ownership and definitions explicit before automating them. I connect process, systems, data and reporting as one operating model because treating them as separate projects usually creates new gaps between them.

I prefer visible trade-offs and named decision rights. I will challenge a weak assumption, but I also know a theoretically perfect model is useless if the people running it cannot use it under real pressure.

I document the system and hand it back. The aim is not to create a function that is indispensable because nobody else understands it; the aim is to build an operating model the business can run, govern and improve.

## What I would inspect first in a new environment

1. **Commercial definitions** — do Marketing, Sales, Finance and leadership mean the same thing by lead, MQL, opportunity, pipeline, source and forecast?
2. **Ownership and handoffs** — who owns each transition and what happens when a handoff fails?
3. **System authority** — where is each critical truth supposed to live and where has logic been duplicated?
4. **Data quality and reporting** — can the business reconcile operational records to the metrics used in decisions?
5. **Operating load** — what work is repetitive and deterministic, and what work requires senior judgement?
6. **Automation / AI boundary** — which workflows are safe to automate now, which require better context, and where is human approval mandatory?

That sequence is usually more useful than starting with a tool replacement.
