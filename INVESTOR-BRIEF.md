# Investor and Adviser Brief

## The idea I am testing

My founder work starts with the operating experience I have built across Marketing Operations, Revenue Operations and GTM systems.

Across different companies and stages, I kept seeing the same pattern: important work becomes unreliable when ownership, rules, systems, data and feedback are fragmented. The question I am exploring is whether the judgement used to solve those problems can become more repeatable through diagnostics, governed workflows, services and software.

ThinkBud and LYNR are two expressions of that question. They are not separate identities from my career; they are places where I have applied the same operating discipline in different forms.

**Live ventures:** [ThinkBud](https://www.thinkbud.co.uk/) · [LYNR](https://getlynr.com/)

## What exists today

### Operating frameworks

I have built company-neutral frameworks covering Revenue Operating Systems, Lifecycle Governance, Campaign Operations, Pipeline Truth and Modern Marketing Operations.

The aim is to make decisions, ownership, operating rules and governance inspectable and reusable.

### AI-assisted operating architecture

The [GTM Command Center](skills/command-center/README.md) is the clearest expression of how I think about AI in Operations.

It is designed to:

- Clarify the decision being made
- Separate the visible symptom from the likely root cause
- Check whether the available evidence is sufficient
- Route the work to the right specialist analysis
- Keep assumptions, conflicts and uncertainty visible
- Require a named human decision or escalation

I have not turned every historical skill name into a polished product page. Where the source is not verified, I say so. I would rather show a smaller amount of credible work than manufacture a large AI catalogue.

### Runnable proof

I built two small tools using fictional data:

- [Pipeline Quality Scanner](tools/pipeline-quality-scanner/README.md)
- [Lifecycle Transition Validator](tools/lifecycle-validator/README.md)

They are not products. They show that parts of the operating judgement can be expressed as explicit rules, tested and reviewed rather than remaining as advice in a document.

## Founder builds that extend the core work

### ThinkBud

ThinkBud began as something I wanted to create for my own children. As I worked on it, the problem became broader: how could a learning product use practice history, spaced review and structured content to make daily preparation more useful and more trustworthy?

The [ThinkBud case](case-studies/ventures/building-thinkbud.md) explains the build in detail. The live site is [www.thinkbud.co.uk](https://www.thinkbud.co.uk/).

Over nearly eight months, I took it from an idea to a working, invite-only learning-intelligence platform. The build spans product and learning-model design, adaptive practice, event-sourced learner data, parent and learner journeys, secure answer handling, subscriptions, testing and controlled release.

This is evidence that I can transfer my core operating discipline into a real software product while retaining responsibility for the product, architecture, evidence standards and release boundaries.

### LYNR

The [Building LYNR retrospective](case-studies/ventures/building-lynr.md) explains how I applied the same operator experience to a senior revenue-execution model. The live site is [getlynr.com](https://getlynr.com/).

LYNR is built around clear diagnosis, defined scope, one accountable lead, senior execution and a clean handback. It shows how the same operating judgement can become a service model rather than software.

Together, ThinkBud and LYNR demonstrate two forms of founder execution: one as a product and one as a delivery model.

## What I believe is reusable

The durable asset is not a particular CRM setup, a list of prompts or access to a model.

It is the combination of:

1. Recognising the operating pattern behind a visible symptom
2. Making the decision and ownership explicit
3. Defining the evidence needed before acting
4. Designing one authoritative record of what happened
5. Keeping human accountability in place
6. Reusing frameworks without pretending every context is the same
7. Testing logic through working software and synthetic tools
8. Applying senior judgement during implementation
