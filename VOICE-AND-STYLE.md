# Voice and Style Standard

This file sets the writing standard for every reviewer-facing page in this repository.

## The voice

The writing should sound like me explaining my work directly to an intelligent colleague, hiring manager or investor.

It should be:

- First person
- Direct
- Practical
- Commercially aware
- Comfortable with detail
- Honest about uncertainty
- Clear without being simplistic
- Human rather than polished for the sake of polish

## Point of view

Use **I**, **my** and **I built** when describing my experience, judgement, decisions or work.

Use **you** only when guiding a reviewer through the repository.

Do not describe me as “Christopher”, “he” or “his” in reviewer-facing narrative. Third person is allowed only in a factual table, quoted source or formal governance record where it is genuinely clearer.

## Words and patterns to avoid

Do not use generic AI or consultancy language such as:

- AI-powered
- Cutting-edge
- Revolutionary
- Transformative
- Seamless
- Future-proof
- Best-in-class
- World-class
- Unlock value
- Leverage synergies
- Drive innovation
- Thought leader
- Game-changing
- Robust solution
- End-to-end excellence
- Unique blend
- Unparalleled expertise

Words such as **scalable**, **strategic**, **innovative**, **platform**, **architecture** and **transformation** are allowed only when the sentence explains what they mean in practice.

## Writing rules

1. Lead with the business problem or decision.
2. Use concrete verbs: diagnose, define, build, test, govern, simplify, remove, reconcile, document and hand back.
3. Explain what changed, not just what was “delivered”.
4. Name the trade-off, constraint or failure mode.
5. Separate what I know from what I believe or am still testing.
6. Avoid claims that could apply to any senior operator.
7. Keep paragraphs natural. Do not create a series of dramatic one-line statements.
8. Use bullets where they improve scanning, not as a substitute for explanation.
9. Explain specialist terms the first time they appear.
10. Prefer a narrower claim I can defend over a stronger claim I cannot prove.

## Before and after

### Avoid

> Christopher is a visionary operator who leverages cutting-edge AI to unlock scalable GTM transformation.

### Use

> I use AI where the decision, evidence and accountable owner are clear. I do not use it to hide weak process or unreliable data.

### Avoid

> This framework delivers a robust, future-proof revenue architecture.

### Use

> This framework makes ownership, handoffs, system authority and reporting rules explicit so the model can be governed as the company changes.

## Final test

Before publishing a page, read it aloud and ask:

- Would I actually say this in an interview or working session?
- Does the sentence explain something useful?
- Is the language more impressive than the evidence?
- Could this paragraph have been written about almost anyone?
- Have I hidden uncertainty behind polished wording?

Where the answer is uncomfortable, rewrite it.
